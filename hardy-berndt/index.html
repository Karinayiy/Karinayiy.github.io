EXPERIMENT 2 · POINTWISE GROWTH

Is 𝒮(c) positive, and how fast does it grow?

Conjecture 6.12 (𝒮(c) ≥ 0) and Question 6.13 (pointwise asymptotics)

The paper asks whether 𝒮(c) = (c/4)(log c − log 2 + γ) + o(c). The second view subtracts the main term (c log c)/4 and divides by c; if the proposed formula holds, the points should settle at (γ − log 2)/4 ≈ −0.0290.

EXPERIMENT 5 · FOURIER REPRESENTATION

A conditionally convergent series for S₂(c)

Theorem 6.10: S₂(c) = (4/π) Σj odd, j<c (j/c − ½) Σl odd (1/l) cot(πlj/(2c))

The inner series converges only in its natural order, by Dirichlet's test, not absolutely. The chart shows the partial sums over l ≤ L against the exact value from the literal sum. Convergence is slow and oscillating, which is why the paper does not use this formula for the asymptotics.

The O(c) term remembers c mod small odd numbers

A follow-up to Question 6.13, not a result of either paper

Experiment 2 shows that K(c) = 𝒮(c)/c − (log c)/4 keeps a spread of about ±0.115 that does not shrink. Grouping c by its residue mod q shows where much of that spread comes from. The pattern suggests one predictor built from the odd divisors of c + 1 and c − 1:

F(c) = Σd | c+1, d odd φ(d)/d² − Σd | c−1, d odd φ(d)/d² This is an empirical guess, chosen because it matches the residue pattern above. Nothing in either paper predicts it.
